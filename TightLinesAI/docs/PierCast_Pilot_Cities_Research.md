## 2. How to interpret the evidence

- **Direct report:** An agency specifically identifies pier catches at the named port. This supports occurrence during that reporting period, not a full-month peak or an abundance estimate.
- **Local/grouped listing:** An agency identifies the fishery, sometimes grouping multiple ports. Useful foundational evidence, with transfer limits retained.
- **Historical:** Older direct evidence establishes a research lead; it cannot establish current local strength without review.
- **Regional inference:** A proposed seasonal research window transferred from comparable evidence. It must be reviewed before becoming a production curve.
- **Unresolved:** This pass did not find adequate support for the specific species, covered structure, or month. Do not assign zero or a low score to this state.

Report dates are preserved. A weekly bulletin can describe days in the preceding month; the CSV uses the publication month as an evidence bin, not an exact biological onset date. Reported low catches and unsuccessful trips also matter; finding one positive bulletin does not justify ignoring negative weeks.

Fishery seasons below mean biological opportunity research windows. They are not legal open seasons. Access, lawful targeting, harvest rules, and permitted methods need separate, current jurisdiction review.

## 5. Year-round implementation implications

1. **Keep every month represented.** Unknown winter biology or provider coverage yields an explicit unavailable capability, not a summer curve carried forward.
2. **Start species-specific seasonal profiles only where evidence supports a distinction.** Spring versus staging Chinook and spring versus fall steelhead are research contexts, not automatic extra multipliers.
3. **Retain episodic summer salmonids.** Direct June/July pier catches show why a hard “salmon only in fall” calendar would be wrong. Quantifying those opportunities still requires representative temperature and calibration.
4. **Do not fill empty months with invented species.** Warm-season drum, smallmouth, catfish, and perch matter where locally supported; other cities may legitimately have fewer targets.
5. **Separate catch occurrence from catchability curves.** These sources support roster/season research. They do not supply temperature optima, score ceilings, coefficients, response lags, or catch probabilities.
6. **Use explicit winter access states.** Historical ice/snow restrictions demonstrate the need for route and structure checks; an old report does not establish today's closure. [A12]
7. **Do not train on search snippets.** They repeatedly combine a heading from one city with a species sentence from another. Read the full city paragraph and preserve fishing mode.

### Completed annual-biology phase and continuing evidence standard

**Every retained species has now been reviewed across all twelve months and its relevant seasonal behaviors.** The resulting profiles and explicit gaps are in the annual biology document. The evidence standard below continues to govern any future parameter adoption; completion of the review is not approval of a year-round favorite-temperature band or numerical curve.

For each species × seasonal context, parameter adoption must record:

1. Life stage and behavior being modeled: feeding, migration, staging, spawning or overwintering only where applicable and supported. Separate habitat occupancy from willingness to bite.
2. Month-by-month applicability at each city, including uncertain transitions. Months organize the review; supported biological phases determine curves. Use smooth transitions and do not force twelve unique curves.
3. Whether an observation measures selected habitat, feeding/growth performance, spawning conditions, acclimation response or tolerance. These are different quantities; survival limits and spawning temperatures cannot be relabeled angling optima.
4. Absolute temperature response, warming/cooling trend, recent thermal history and response lag **only when evidence supports their use**. Record unsupported effects as unknown.
5. Reachable water layer/depth and whether the available sensor/model represents it. Surface temperature cannot automatically stand in for harbor bottom or offshore habitat.
6. Which effect belongs to seasonal presence versus conditional thermal suitability. Avoid counting migration/seasonality twice or fitting a temperature curve to calendar-driven presence.
7. Regional/population applicability, contradictions, source strength and calibration assumptions. Equal temperatures may produce different seasonal predictions, but a difference must have an evidentiary basis.
8. Explicit January–December review outcomes: supported profile, justified shared profile, biological evidence missing, or environmental input unavailable. Lack of winter data is not a low score.

Do not begin numerical threshold selection from isolated catch-report temperatures. Those are observations with uncontrolled effort, depth and other conditions, useful for later validation rather than stand-alone preference curves.

## 8. What this research does not authorize

This pass defines a defensible starting inventory and points out evidence gaps. It does not enable production forecasts, certify live access, select model cells, or supply calibrated temperatures and scores. The listed source references must be expanded into the master spec's complete parameter-level evidence records before configuration publication.
