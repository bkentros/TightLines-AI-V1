# Betsie River Run Copy Foundation

**Status:** Geography approved; bundled copy renovation implemented and mechanically accepted; owner copy review pending
**Version:** `betsie-copy-foundation-v1`
**Researched:** 2026-08-10
**Applies to:** Betsie Fall Chinook, Fall Coho, and Fall Steelhead

## 1. Product-owner geography decision

The Betsie's River Run corridor should not be forced into Lower, Middle, and
Upper river sections. The usable product corridor is short, Homestead is the
upstream endpoint for River Run guidance, and a three-section model would add
false precision.

Approved public geography (owner approved 2026-08-10):

1. **Lake and harbor context** — Lake Michigan, Frankfort harbor, and Betsie
   Lake. This is staging context, not proof that fish have entered the river.
2. **Betsie Lake–US-31 reach** — the river from its Betsie Lake transition to
   the US-31 bridge.
3. **US-31–Homestead reach** — the river from the US-31 bridge to the current
   signed Homestead closure. The closure boundary, not the structure, is the
   legal upstream endpoint of any fishing recommendation.

These exact labels are normative for Betsie River Run copy. Do not replace them
with Lower, Middle, Upper, `lakeward`, or a generic Homestead approach.

## 2. River identity and corridor

- River ID: `betsie`
- Public name: Betsie River
- State: Michigan
- Timezone: `America/Detroit`
- Flow direction: generally west into Betsie Lake, which connects to Lake
  Michigan at Frankfort and Elberta.
- Whole mainstem: approximately 52 miles from Green Lake to Betsie Lake.
- River Run fall-migration guidance corridor: Betsie Lake to the signed
  Homestead closure.
- Product upstream endpoint: legal water below Homestead for all three
  supported runs. River Run does not recommend fall migratory water above the
  structure.

Authoritative geography support:

- [Michigan DNR Betsie Natural River Plan](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/Fisheries/NaturalRivers/Betsie_River_Plan.pdf?rev=695726bbe46b406d8f208273792b80bd)
  describes the river entering Betsie Lake, the 52-mile mainstem, the
  below-Homestead salmon and Steelhead fishery, and US-31 as a major crossing.
- [Michigan DNR Fisheries Report 24](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/DNRFishLibrary/FisheriesReports/FR024.pdf)
  defines its lower survey segment as Betsie Lake to US-31 and identifies
  US-31, Homestead, Grace Road, River Road, and other survey locations.
- [Michigan DNR forest compartment material](https://www2.dnr.state.mi.us/PUBLICATIONS/pdfs/ForestsLandWater/Cmpt_Reviews/Traverse_City/2018/tra027_comp_info.pdf)
  specifically identifies Homestead–US-31 as heavily fished seasonal salmon and
  Steelhead water.

### Public-name instruction

Avoid the naked phrase `river mouth`. The Betsie River enters Betsie Lake,
while Betsie Lake reaches Lake Michigan through the Frankfort/Elberta harbor
channel. Depending on the reader, `mouth` can refer to either transition.

Use:

- `Betsie Lake and the lake-to-river transition`
- `Betsie Lake–US-31 reach`
- `US-31–Homestead reach`
- `the current signed Homestead closure`

Do not use:

- `lower / middle / upper Betsie` in River Run state guidance
- `lakeward end` without naming Betsie Lake
- `toward Homestead` as the entire location plan
- `legal Homestead approach` without explaining that the signed closure is the
  endpoint
- `above Homestead` as recommended migratory water

## 3. Barrier inventory

### 3.1 Homestead Dam / lamprey barrier and fish-passage facility

- Barrier ID: `betsie_homestead`
- Official public description: Homestead Dam lamprey barrier and fish-passage
  facility
- Location: 44.596362, -86.079163; DNR Betsie River Access Site, 2388 Dam Road,
  Benzonia
- Status: active residual dam/spillway and lamprey barrier; the former
  hydroelectric superstructure was decommissioned and the spillway was rebuilt
  in 1979
- Product corridor role: upstream endpoint for all River Run fall-migration
  recommendations
- Fall closure: fishing is closed within 300 feet of the facility from August
  1 through November 15
- Other-season closure: fishing is closed within 100 feet from November 16
  through July 31
- Copy endpoint: `the current signed Homestead closure`
- Last verified: 2026-08-10

Passage conclusion requires precise wording. The product owner has directed
River Run to treat Homestead as the endpoint of the practical salmon corridor
and to make no recommendations above it. Current Michigan DNR material,
however, says the spillway was rebuilt for **limited fish passage**. Public copy
therefore must not claim that Homestead is absolutely impassable. It should say:

> River Run guidance ends below Homestead Dam.

It must not say:

> Salmon cannot pass Homestead Dam.

Sources:

- [Michigan DNR Homestead access and structure description](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/managing-resources/fisheries/units/c-michigan)
- [2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/LED/digests/2026-Michigan-Fishing-Regulations_web_accessible.pdf?rev=b8a345e6033d4f5e9ddfc8610dec6e1d)
- [Michigan DNR Fisheries Order 204 background](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/Boards/NRC/2020/folder2/Signed_Packet_for_Posting.pdf?rev=c8c255c6f6484fc0ae63b5c4888141a9)

The April 2026 flood activated an emergency action plan. Subsequent reporting
said the structure remained stable after water receded, and the current DNR
unit page still lists the access site. Structure, access, closure, and signed
boundary status must be checked again immediately before public release. The
flood is not converted into a seasonal score or live-condition claim.

### 3.2 Upstream structures outside the public corridor

- Thompsonville Dam failed in 1989 and its remnants were later removed.
- Grass Lake Dam remains far upstream in the headwater system.

Neither structure belongs in ordinary Betsie River Run copy because public
guidance ends at Homestead. They remain in the research record so an agent does
not misidentify them as current boundaries of the below-Homestead product
corridor.

## 4. Approved-source coverage proposal

### Hydraulics

No continuous live hydraulic source is accepted for the River Run corridor.
USGS location 04126600 at US-31 has field measurements but no continuous or
daily data. It cannot support today's Push, Fishability, or cumulative Migration
Timing.

Source: [USGS 04126600 inventory](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/monitoring-location/USGS-04126600/)

### Measured water temperature

No accepted live measured-water-temperature source represents the Betsie Lake
to Homestead corridor. Air temperature must not be substituted.

### Weather

The configured Open-Meteo point at Homestead supplies hourly light, cloud, and
precipitation context for the explicitly weather-only Activity Outlook. It does
not observe flow, river level, clarity, or water temperature. Its conclusion is
weather support for responsiveness if fish are present, not a whole-river
condition report.

## 6. Current-copy defect audit

### 6.1 Migration Stage

The current strategy understands that the corridor is short, but many states
still make the user decode a moving set of vague phrases:

- `lakeward end`
- `first substantial corridor holes`
- `toward Homestead`
- `legal Homestead approach`
- `proven corridor holes`
- `deep established water`

These are habitat or tactic descriptions, not stable geographic answers. They
also make `WHERE TO START` too long and cause the apparent starting plan to
jump between states.

Required correction:

- Every state chooses one of the two approved named reaches.
- At most one secondary reach is named with a clear trigger.
- Habitat and tactics move to `GUIDE'S READ`.
- The lake/harbor context is used only before dependable river entry.
- The signed Homestead closure is persistent regulation/scope copy rather than
  repeated filler in every location sentence.

Blatantly wrong current copy:

> Earlier arrivals have had time to pass the structure...

This appears in Betsie Fall Steelhead's established Building state. It
contradicts the product corridor, conflicts with the surrounding below-Homestead
guidance, and is too ambiguous even under DNR's limited-passage description.
It must be removed. QA must reject `pass the structure`, not merely
`pass upstream` or `above Homestead`.

Terminal defects:

- Chinook and Coho offseason copy says to target another species rather than
  showing `Fall run complete` plus the species-specific staging return point.
- Steelhead currently exposes a Winter holding state and retained 61/100 score.
  That conflicts with the accepted `Fall entry complete` behavior while a
  winter experience is not implemented.

### 6.2 Migration Timing

Only the Unavailable state is reachable. Its current Guide's Read says not to
`move upstream or stay lower`, which imports a generic long-river mental model
that does not fit the Betsie.

Required correction: explain simply that the missing long-term flow and
measured-temperature record prevents an Ahead/Typical/Delayed comparison and
therefore cannot shift the user between the two Betsie reaches.

### 6.3 Push

Only the Unavailable state is reachable. The core meaning is correct, but the
copy is generic and wordy.

Required correction: say that the Betsie lacks the representative live flow
and measured water-temperature inputs needed to judge fresh movement. Do not
borrow another river or air temperature. Do not imply that weather-only rain
means the river rose.

### 6.4 Fishability

Only the Unavailable state is reachable. The core meaning is correct.

Required correction: state that without representative continuous flow,
FinFindr cannot judge presentation shape in either Betsie reach. Direct
observation and current local guidance are the fallback; no copy may claim safe
wading, boating, floating, or access.

### 6.5 Activity Outlook

Current rendered details commonly combine seven or more claims in one long
paragraph:

- overall responsiveness
- one strongest block
- light driver
- rain limitation
- stage/lifecycle biology
- corridor scope
- weather-only input disclosure
- three missing river inputs

This is the clearest example of the dropdown carrying too much copy. The same
scope and confidence limitations repeat in the headline, detail, and guide.

Required correction:

- Keep `weather-only` and `Limited confidence` visible.
- Use at most three Why points: overall meaning, leading block(s), and the one
  material limitation or lifecycle qualification.
- Keep the persistent scope note outside the rotating Why points.
- Apply the PM-approved three-point leader rule. A single strongest block needs
  at least a three-point lead; near-tied leaders must both be named.
- Add Betsie fixtures for singular leader, tied leaders, missing hourly
  weather, staging, salmon taper/ending, and Steelhead fall-entry completion.
- Remove repeated `verify ... safe access` language. Activity is not a safety
  determination.
- Stop producing a current Betsie Steelhead Activity score after `Fall entry
  complete`; no winter experience is implemented yet.

### 6.6 Fish In River

The current direction and relative-strength concepts are mostly correct, but
the same seasonal-estimate disclaimer is repeated through detail and guide,
and generic `broad accessible river` wording ignores the short two-reach model.

Required correction:

- Use the shared five-point public quantization standard while preserving raw
  engine values.
- Keep the meter and text on the same displayed value.
- Describe distribution within the two-reach River Run corridor, not the full
  Betsie mainstem.
- Move the permanent `seasonal estimate—not a live count` boundary out of
  repeated state prose.
- Render `Fall run complete` and `Fall entry complete` with no score or meter
  marker.
- Do not retain 61/100 as a current winter Steelhead value.

rounding, and terminal score removal. Older audits remain historical evidence,
not proof of current copy.
