## 1. Portfolio decision

All 16 candidate combinations pass the recurrence threshold independently. None
is included merely because another salmonid uses the river. The evidence does
not support equal treatment. Bois Brule Coho and Steelhead have the strongest
current fishway counts, while Root fall Steelhead strength is a locally
calibrated opportunity rating that cannot be inferred from the facility's short
fall operating sample.

No additional species is admitted. Lake trout, pink salmon, brook trout, and
other fish appearing in agency material remain outside this cohort because they
were not requested or independently researched through this gate.

## 3. Relative-scale controls

The proposed ceilings are ordinal portfolio judgments, not conversions from
stocking or fishway totals. They preserve these comparisons:

- Root Coho and Bois Brule Steelhead remain below the existing `10/10` Platte
  Coho reference.
- Root Chinook and Bois Brule Coho align with the accepted `8/10` Grand Coho
  benchmark where current direct return evidence warrants it.
- Milwaukee and Sheboygan owner calibrations exceed several research priors.
  Their medium confidence must remain visible because stocking establishes
  recurrence, not the number of adults returning to a river.
- Root Steelhead is `7/10` for fall opportunity based on owner-relayed local
  field knowledge. The facility's limited fall sample cannot override that
  calibration or quantify its ceiling. The engine must still preserve the
  different strain/run phases rather than displaying one flat fall-to-spring
  run.
- Bois Brule has the strongest direct Brown Trout evidence in this cohort even
  though the owner selected Milwaukee 9, Sheboygan 8, Root 7, and Bois Brule 7.
  Product calibration does not reorder the evidence-quality hierarchy.
- Sheboygan's Brown ceiling is not inferred from its large stocking allocation
  alone. Stocking, current county creel, and direct agency reach listings
  combine to establish support, while the lack of a return counter limits
  confidence.
- Distribution and strength remain independent. Broad distribution does not
  independently imply either a high or low opportunity ceiling.

## 4. Endpoint findings and resolved contradiction

### Source-quality confidence audit

Agency material establishes recurrence, biology, timing, and measurable
constraints: current facility or fishway counts where available, then brood
samples, stocking records, creel estimates, and reach guidance. Owner-relayed
local field knowledge sets the revised opportunity ceilings. It is recorded as
calibration evidence—not converted into a fish count and never used by itself to
establish species presence.

| Rivers              | Current strength basis                                                                                                | Confidence consequence                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Root and Bois Brule | Direct 2024–2025 DNR facility/fishway observations and multi-year agency series, plus owner-relayed local calibration | High confidence in recurrence; Root's exact fall Steelhead ceiling remains medium-confidence because its facility sample is not a census |
| Milwaukee           | 2024 DNR stocking, passage/brood observations, and county/mode creel estimates                                        | Medium confidence where no complete river return count exists; owner-selected high ceilings do not increase evidence confidence          |
| Sheboygan           | 2024 DNR stocking and county/mode creel estimates, plus direct river reach guidance                                   | Medium or medium-low confidence; no current return counter, so stocking never independently establishes strength                         |

Root and Bois Brule research priors are count-backed, but Root's fall Steelhead
facility totals are not a valid whole-run abundance measure. Milwaukee and
Sheboygan remain lower-confidence because current stocking and access evidence
cannot be treated as adult-return abundance. The locally informed,
owner-selected ratings are product calibrations layered on agency evidence; they
do not erase these limitations.

### Root River

The Gate 2 conservative endpoint remains correct for the initial product, but
its original biological rationale needs qualification. The fall 2025 facility
summary directly reports **3,372 Chinook and 5,459 Coho passed upstream**, so
the facility is not an absolute biological endpoint during staffed operations.
Horlick Dam remains the outer biological barrier.

Gate 3 does not silently expand the owner-approved three-section product. V1
still ends at the downstream face of the Steelhead Facility because passage is
operational, species/count dependent, and the reach above it was not approved as
a product section. Public copy must say that DNR may pass processed fish
upstream; it must not say the facility permanently blocks every fish.

### Milwaukee River

Kletzsch is the species-truth endpoint for Chinook, Coho, and Steelhead in V1.
Current passage monitoring provides some Steelhead evidence there, but reviewed
modern sources do not prove recurring Chinook or Coho opportunity throughout
every upstream structure in the wider physical corridor. The model therefore
fails closed at Kletzsch rather than treating fish-passage infrastructure as
proof of unrestricted distribution.

### Sheboygan and Bois Brule rivers

Sheboygan's three approved sections through the Waelderhaus/Kohler reach are
supported by agency fall-location guidance. Bois Brule's candidates are confined
to the approved lower-river corridor ending at Highway 2 and the lamprey-barrier
boundary; no guidance may imply passage above that boundary.

## 5. Brown Trout engine boundary

Lake-run Brown Trout requires its own engine/profile family. It may share
provider plumbing and generic score presentation, but it may not inherit
Chinook/Coho terminal mortality logic, Steelhead calendars, or another species'
temperature thresholds.

- Brown Trout are iteroparous: they do not automatically die after spawning.
  Lake Michigan DNR guidance says some return to the lake depending on age and
  condition, while tributary guidance also documents fish remaining through
  winter. The engine must model an uncertain post-spawn split—holding versus
  downstream return—not a universal outcome or population disappearance.
- Lake Michigan Seeforellen timing is later than the salmon calendars. Current
  brood collections from Milwaukee and Root occurred weekly from Nov. 20 to Dec.
  17, while DNR describes ripe collection in November and early December.
- Bois Brule is a separate Lake Superior calendar family. Current DNR guidance
  sets Brown entry from early July through late October with a mid-July through
  mid-September peak, not a copied Lake Michigan November curve. The reviewed
  page explicitly attributes overwintering to Steelhead, so Brown copy must not
  claim an equally documented Bois Brule winter-holding phase.
- Presence ceilings remain ordinal seasonal-opportunity calibrations. Stocking,
  creel harvest, brood samples, and fishway totals are retained as distinct
  evidence types and are never converted directly into a score.
- Activity weights, water-temperature response, lifecycle transitions, and
  weather-only behavior remain unresolved until Gate 4 replay and owner review.

The missing-temperature contract is separately documented in
[`wisconsin-2026-no-temperature-contract.md`](./wisconsin-2026-no-temperature-contract.md).
