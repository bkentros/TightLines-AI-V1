# Pass 2 handoff — five-city species and scoring audit

Scope: Two Rivers, Kewaunee, Algoma, Manitowoc, Wisconsin; Waukegan, Illinois. This handoff accompanies the [corrected Pass 1 report](PASS1_REPORT.md), [95-pair decision matrix](species-decisions.json), [source ledger](source-ledger.json), and [out-of-catalog leads](out-of-catalog-leads.json). All five cities remain private until the owner explicitly chooses to go live.

## Mistake to avoid

The first Pass 1 shortlist advanced only 10 city/species pairings. I used the evidence threshold for **approving a numeric score** to decide which species deserved **research**. I also read the Wisconsin DNR's 2022–24 county pier lake-trout zeros without checking the older positive years in the same table. That prematurely excluded lake trout and held common salmonids at several ports. The owner caught the omission. The corrected review advances **32** pairings for research, holds **12** occurrence leads, and leaves **51** without a credible local pier lead in the reviewed collection. Those counts are research dispositions, not public rosters or numeric admissions.

Future agents must read the existing [candidate-roster correction](../remaining-species/CANDIDATE_ROSTER.md) before screening species. Recurring port pier/shore harvest, a named city-harbor report, or a credible exact-pier specialist account can justify research even when the exact structure's magnitude is unresolved. Retain the geography, date, mode, and uncertainty on every claim. Do not remove a species from the research queue merely because it lacks score-ready interviews. Conversely, do not turn a research candidate into a score from county totals, a single anecdote, stocking, electrofishing, or charter catch.

## Mandatory Pass 2 coverage

1. Re-open **every row** in the 95-pair matrix, including the 12 held leads. Audit all owner-named species explicitly: Chinook, coho, steelhead, brown trout, lake trout, perch, smallmouth, pike, lake/round whitefish, and smelt. Smelt is outside the current 19-species catalog and needs its own fishing-method and legal-season decision. Do not assume the same roster at all five ports.
2. Give **lake trout at all five cities** a dedicated evidence section. Wisconsin DNR's county pier table shows older positive harvest in both Kewaunee and Manitowoc counties, but 2022–24 estimates are zero; it pools two target cities per county. Seek current city-harbor pier/shore observations and cold-season evidence before deciding strength. Illinois DNR says winter shore-structure lake trout are possible at some sites, but does not name Waukegan Government Pier. Do not borrow offshore or reef strength for any pier. [WI DNR county table](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/sites/default/files/topic/Fishing/LM_CreelHarvestTables1998-2024.pdf); [Illinois DNR lake page](https://ifishillinois.org/Waterbodies/Details/76b7c9b3-4594-4e64-8651-49b0afbdc657).
3. Distinguish city harbor/main public piers from upriver, offshore, launched boat, charter, and neighboring port data. Use county or lakewide values as context with an explicit allocation limit. Treat historical exact-pier reports as occurrence evidence; verify current magnitude independently. For Algoma, continue the full fishery report and encode construction as access context without claiming that a closed breakwater segment is open.
4. For each score-ready pairing, build evidence-backed **365-day opportunity modes**. Mark sampled low catch, no sampling, legal closure, likely biological absence, and unknown as different conditions. Establish the onset, prime window, decline, and credible short weather/temperature-driven pulses from multiple seasons where possible. Do not spread a spring or fall peak across months that lack support.
5. Apply the [Formula v3 decision](../scoring-v3-pass2/FORMULA_V3_DECISION.md) and the **latest** [September 2026 seasonal opportunity recalibration](../seasonal-opportunity-audit-2026-09/README.md): absolute cross-city fishery strength `F`, recurring availability `A`, and nearshore thermal fit `T`; the strongest supported mode determines the score, bounded by `1 ≤ score ≤ seasonalPotential ≤ F ≤ 10`. The latest audit corrected 17 pairs, including October lake trout and strong seasonal shoulders, and its after-matrix is the comparison baseline. A genuinely elite port and species can have a high ceiling. Let well-supported prime windows approach their reviewed ceiling; keep weak species or off-season periods appropriately low. The desired aggressive prime-time behavior is **earned by evidence**, not a uniform bonus. Compare peaks and full-year curves against Sheboygan and existing calibrated ports on the same absolute scale.
6. Produce a city × species evidence and score audit with source links, evidence grade, numerical anchor, seasonal modes, peak timing, off-season behavior, legal treatment, access note, and reason for each held/unscored pairing. Inspect all 365 dates and representative thermal scenarios for boundedness, plausible peaks, and abrupt artifacts. Keep new cities and scores private for owner review; public promotion is a later explicit decision.

The owner expects complete reports for all five cities. A remaining weak species should be shown as an investigated lead with a clear reason for no numeric score, rather than silently disappearing or receiving a fabricated score.
