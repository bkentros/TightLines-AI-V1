# PierCast species roster for batch score research

This is the current research queue. The earlier 24 exclusions / 18 unresolved / three narrow leads applied a major-target and scoring-readiness standard too early. Those assessments remain useful caveats, but they do not determine whether a species deserves research. This roster admits credible recurring pier catches more broadly, as instructed, and keeps all numerical and public-release gates unchanged.

**25 city × species candidates** enter score research together. Seven weaker occurrence leads need verification; 13 pairings have no established local pier evidence in the reviewed collection. These are evidence-planning decisions, not claims that all 25 are major fisheries or verified at every covered structure.

## Direct reports and remaining distinctions

- **Ludington:** Smallmouth, drum and perch have repeated local pier corroboration. Walleye and lake trout have thinner but multi-year port catches, so enter as cautious candidates. Stub-pier-only smallmouth records remain excluded from North Breakwater attribution. Lake whitefish is a sparse occurrence lead; round whitefish has an older recurrence signal.
- **Grand Haven:** All nine enter research. Smallmouth, drum, catfish, largemouth and round whitefish have species-specific pier reports alongside creel recurrence. Perch, walleye and lake trout retain port/dock and recency limitations. Lake whitefish requires lawful-method calibration after the November gear change; that is a scoring issue, not a reason to omit the species from research.
- **Manistee:** All except channel catfish enter research. Lake trout is now a cautious candidate because repeated pier reports and port records show catches, though some named catches belong to the excluded South Pier. Lake whitefish and largemouth enter with sparse-report cautions. Perch, walleye, smallmouth, drum and round whitefish retain their individual seasonal leads.
- **Frankfort–Elberta:** Lake trout, smallmouth and drum enter as provisional port-mode candidates. Their repeated Pier/Dock records justify checking exact breakwater use; offshore lake-trout reports do not supply that confirmation. Walleye remains a lead because only one modern positive port year was found and current reports describe trolling. Lake whitefish is similarly sparse; round whitefish is historical.
- **Sheboygan:** Smallmouth and perch remain occurrence leads, supported respectively by a dated local pier report and historical recollection. Regional Wisconsin pier totals do not verify either city pier. The remaining seven are open evidence gaps, not demonstrated ecological absences.

Each pairing’s source links, publication dates, original fishing mode and geographic limitations are preserved in `sources.json` and `candidate-roster.json`. See the [detailed prior evidence review](README.md) for source-level discussion; its major-target classifications are not this queue.

## Work through the roster as a batch

1. Research each species across all five cities, using the same source collection and retaining separate city findings. Verify the seven occurrence leads alongside the 25 candidates rather than waiting for one pairing to be finished.
2. For every candidate, establish seasonal timing and relative strength. Carry exact-pier, recency and incidental-catch uncertainty into the assessment instead of dropping the candidate before examining its season.
3. Research one appropriate thermal evidence base per species, then assess local applicability. Configure city-specific seasonal curves together wherever justified; do not require serial one-pier onboarding.
4. Preserve unavailable periods and unsupported pairings. Candidate admission does not authorize fabricated decimals, copied seasonal curves, new structures, a changed formula or public release.

The completed Chinook, coho, steelhead and brown-trout configuration remains unchanged. This update is research planning only; no runtime change, migration or deployment is needed.
