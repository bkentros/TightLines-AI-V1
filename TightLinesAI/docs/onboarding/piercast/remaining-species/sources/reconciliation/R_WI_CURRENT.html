Lake Michigan Outdoor Fishing Report - Sept. 7, 2026

Fishing Wisconsin

Generally, fishing reports will be updated weekly on Tuesdays. This may change based on the availability of reports and the field staff's work schedules.

Unless otherwise indicated, this fishing report information comes from our creel survey clerks, who work in the lakeshore areas from March through October and in the tributary rivers seasonally in spring and fall. Creel clerk schedules are random and vary in the days and times they are scheduled to be in a specific location. This allows us to generate statistically valid fishing efforts and harvest information.

The information in these reports is based on the days and times of the week that creel clerks are present. You can obtain additional fishing information by contacting local bait shops, charter captains, guides and marinas.

Reminder: Anglers must carry a paper copy of their license/stamps while fishing on Lake Michigan, Lake Superior and the Wisconsin/Michigan, Wisconsin/Minnesota and Wisconsin/Iowa boundary waters.

Sept. 7, 2026: West Shore Green Bay Fishing Report

Brown County

Duck Creek: Anglers fishing Duck Creek reported catching many yellow perch with both minnows and nightcrawlers. Most yellow perch were small, but anglers were able to catch a limit of perch averaging about 7 inches long. Anglers also caught a few small bluegills.

Fox River: Fox River shore anglers have been catching a few white perch and channel catfish near the river's mouth using nightcrawlers. Anglers fishing the Fox River from boats reported catching a few small to medium sized channel catfish on paddle tails and jerkbaits. Most boaters interviewed from Fox River boat launches were recreational boaters. Fox River temperatures ranged from 75 to 77 degrees Fahrenheit throughout the week. 

Suamico River: Anglers launching from the Suamico River and Geano Beach were successfully catching yellow perch near Pensaukee in 9 to 15 feet of water with live baits such as nightcrawlers and minnows. Anglers were catching many small yellow perch, but most boats were able to catch their limit of larger perch ranging from 9 to 12 inches long within 3 to 4 hours. Anglers targeting musky around Pensaukee Reef reported between 0 to 2 musky per trip with most fish being caught in 12 to 17 feet of water on larger perch imitation lures. Water temperatures around this area ranged from 71 to 73 degrees Fahrenheit. Shore anglers fishing at the Suamico boat launch are catching yellow perch between 4 to 6 inches long on nightcrawlers. Temperatures in the Suamico River have ranged from 72 to 75 degrees Fahrenheit.

Marinette County

Fishing activity was rather light this week with weather being an issue. Some days had rain and thunderstorms along with days of heavy wind scattered throughout the week. Anglers that were out were finding good action for walleyes mainly around Green Island. Casting crawlers on various presentations was best. There were days when boats were headed out at sunset to fish after dark for walleyes as well. Anglers trolling for trout and salmon were still finding fish, but the bite was a little harder this week. Northeast winds pushed the cooler water deeper, so most fishing was taking place deeper than 60 feet of water. A mix of Chinook, rainbows and browns were caught. Water temperatures were in the low 70s.

Oconto County

Scattered rain, thunderstorms and wind kept fishing activity lower on a few days this week. Fish were still being caught in good numbers on better weather days. Summer patterns were still holding up going into September. Rock piles with weeds around them were good places for a mixed bag of perch and walleye. Slip bobbers with crawlers or leeches were working well. Deeper waters of 20 feet plus were producing a mix of perch and walleyes also with slip bobbers and trolling crawler harnesses being popular. Sheepshead, catfish and white perch were also being caught in light numbers while targeting perch and walleyes. Water temperatures were in the mid-70s.

 

Sept. 7, 2026: East Shore Green Bay Fishing Report

Bayshore Park: Moderate to heavy fishing pressure was observed this week. Anglers targeting yellow perch had the best success fishing near Vincent Point in around 15 feet of water using nightcrawlers and live minnows. Anglers targeting walleye had the best success trolling crawler harnesses and Flicker Minnows near Two Mile Reef. Anglers also unintentionally caught drum/sheepshead and white perch. The water temperature was around 75 degrees Fahrenheit.

Eagle's Nest: Moderate fishing activity was observed this week. Anglers targeting yellow perch had the best success fishing near Vincent Point in around 15 feet of water using a mix of nightcrawlers and minnows. Anglers also unintentionally caught white perch and freshwater drum. The water temperature was around 77 degrees Fahrenheit.

Chaudoir’s Dock: Chaudoir's Dock boat launch is now open, although the upper parking lot was still closed. Anglers targeting yellow perch had good success fishing about a mile north of Chaudoir's Dock in around 17 feet of water and farther south near Shoemaker's Point in around 15 feet of water. Anglers targeting walleye had the best success trolling crawler harnesses and minnowbaits near Shoemaker's Point. Anglers also unintentionally caught drum/sheepshead and white perch. The water temperature was around 73 degrees Fahrenheit.

Little Sturgeon Bay: Moderate to heavy fishing pressure was observed this week. Anglers targeting walleye had the best success trolling crawler harnesses and jigging nightcrawlers near Henderson Point and Larsons Reef in 15 to 25 feet of water. Anglers targeting yellow perch had success catching larger-sized perch near Henderson Point in 25 feet of water and within Little Sturgeon Bay in 15 feet of water. Anglers also unintentionally caught freshwater drum and white perch. The water temperature was around 75 degrees Fahrenheit.

Sawyer Harbor: Moderate fishing activity was observed this week. Anglers targeting yellow perch had success fishing within Little Sturgeon Bay and were able to catch some larger-sized yellow perch, although they also caught plenty of smaller ones that were too small to keep. Anglers targeting walleye had great success trolling crawler harnesses and jigging nightcrawlers off Larson's Reef. The water temperature was around 73 degrees Fahrenheit.

 

Sept. 7, 2026: Northern Lake Michigan Fishing Report

Door County

Green Bay: Smallmouth bass remained active throughout several locations. At Ephraim/Anderson Pier, anglers had success targeting smallmouth bass with nightcrawlers, leech-style plastics, and Ned rigs. The bigger fish ranged from 3.5 to 4.5 pounds and 18 to 19 inches. Boat anglers targeting walleye found success using jigs and crawlers in 20 feet of water, with incidental smallmouth bass and sheepshead also caught. Surface water temperature was around 70 degrees Fahrenheit. At Sister Bay, boat anglers had good success targeting smallmouth bass with drop-shot rigs in 8 to 30 feet of water, particularly when anglers had south winds. Water temperature was around 70 degrees Fahrenheit. At Ellison Bay Pier, anglers caught smallmouth bass using Ned rigs, while anglers at Peninsula State Park Pier found success using nightcrawlers. At Egg Harbor Pier, anglers targeted a variety of species including rock bass, white bass, yellow perch, and smallmouth bass, with nightcrawlers producing fish. One angler caught an 8-inch smallmouth bass. Boat anglers at Egg Harbor targeted both walleye and smallmouth bass. Walleye were caught in 8 to 20 feet of water using nightcrawlers and crankbaits, while smallmouth bass were caught using nightcrawlers, drop-shot rigs, Ned rigs and paddletails. Water temperature was around 70 degrees Fahrenheit.

Lake Michigan: Fishing activity on the Lake Michigan side has been slow, with mostly pleasure boaters and some boaters taking advantage of the opening of duck season. Pier anglers continued targeting yellow perch and smallmouth bass, with nightcrawlers producing fish.

Sturgeon Bay: Most shoreline anglers were targeting smallmouth bass, with a few others fishing for yellow perch. Both species were headlined by a nice smallmouth that measured just over 20 inches. A variety of species were reported caught by boaters this week including smallmouth bass, yellow perch, walleye, muskie, freshwater drum, channel catfish and round goby. Smallmouth bass anglers were mainly fishing in 12 to 14 feet of water using soft plastics and worms. Yellow perch boaters were mostly in 12 feet of water using worms and minnows. Walleye were reported to come out in 25 feet of water. Musky were caught in 18 to 20 feet of water. Surface temperatures remained similar to last week, with an average of around 72 degrees Fahrenheit.

Kewaunee County

Algoma: Pier anglers were going after both trout and salmon while anglers fishing the Ahnapee River were mostly fishing for yellow perch. From those surveyed, yellow perch and smallmouth bass were recorded caught with worms being the preferred bait. Water clarity along the river was around a foot of visibility.

Kewaunee: Inside the harbor and out on the pier anglers were fishing for both trout and salmon. Chinook salmon were caught during the week with bloody nose and glow spoons being popular lures. Yellow bullheads were also caught while using worms as bait. Groups fishing on Lake Michigan reported catching rainbow trout and Chinook salmon. Most of the bites came 50 feet down with boaters typically fishing in 90 to 110 feet of water. Surface temperatures were comparable to last week with an average of around 68 degrees Fahrenheit.

Manitowoc County

Earlier in the week anglers had some success catching Chinook salmon, catching on average 3-5 fish per trip. Fish were caught as shallow as 60 feet, with the best results coming from 80-100 feet. Anglers reported bites coming 50-60 feet down. Most of the bites came on spoons. Fishing effort was consistent late in the week with only a few fish caught. Anglers fishing on the piers have had success catching northern pike and a few smallmouth bass. The best baits have been stick-baits and spoons. Anglers on piers have reported around 10 Chinook, with most taken on small spoons. Harbor anglers have caught some bullheads on live bait.

 

Sept. 7, 2026: Southern Lake Michigan Fishing Report

Kenosha County

No report available this week.

Milwaukee County

North Milwaukee: Boats out of McKinley were fishing in a couple different depths depending on if they were out on the lake or inside the break walls. Most boats out on the lake were in 90-120 feet of water, with most of the effort being between 90-100 feet. Boats not out on the lake were fishing primarily the main gap, and the Kinnickinnic River east of the interstate 794 bridge and around the red lighthouse at Lakeshore Stata Park. Most boats out on the lake were trolling and using flasher flies, J-plugs and spoons. Green, blue and glow spoons reportedly got the most action out on the lake away from shore. Boats fishing close to shore reported using some J-plugs but mostly were using jigging spoons and the 3/4 ounce spoon was mentioned repeatedly at the McKinley cleaning station. Shore-casters as a whole increased in number as the bite picked up over the last weekend and the amount of people fishing near the Red Lighthouse at Lakeshore Park increased significantly. For the first time in months fishing from shore in these areas got a little crowded at times. Spoons and jigging spoons made up most lures from shore, with 3/4 ounce spoons being the most popular. Silver, yellow with black spots, white and blue seemed to be the most popular colors and 3/4 ounce was the most popular weight. Some anglers in the Lakeshore lagoon area were targeting panfish but not landing any in significant numbers. A few anglers were fishing the newly reopened inlet landing immediately adjacent to the Milwaukee Yacht Club and accessible by sidewalk right off Lincoln Memorial Dr.; these anglers did well with bluegill, and some crappie.

South Milwaukee: Moderate to heavy fishing pressure was observed this week. Anglers targeting walleye had the best success trolling crawler harnesses and jigging nightcrawlers near Henderson Point and Larsons Reef in 15 to 25 feet of water. Anglers targeting yellow perch had success catching larger-sized perch near Henderson Point in 25 feet of water and within Little Sturgeon Bay in 15 feet of water. Anglers also unintentionally caught freshwater drum and white perch. The water temperature was around 75 degrees Fahrenheit.

Ozaukee County

Pier/Shore: Fishing effort remained moderate throughout the past week. On Friday and Saturday anglers had the greatest success in the early morning hours with green and white spoons off the North Pier. Shore fishermen were happy with their fishing trips, limiting out on coho and Chinook salmon on Thursday afternoon. Shore anglers were using fish spawn, light spoons and jigs as bait. Sunday morning was drastically different with many anglers coming in from their fishing trips with no fish. Moderately warm water conditions were recorded throughout the weekend with the middle of the North Pier averaging 71 degrees Fahrenheit and the shoreline off Coal Dock Park averaging 79 degrees Fahrenheit. 

Ramp: Saturday morning saw steady action at the cleaning station, with many anglers bringing in full limits of salmon and trout or reporting they were close to limiting out fishing near the harbor despite the warm water conditions. Sunday fishing success took a turn with most anglers coming in with 0-2 fish, despite the few anglers who had reached their limits fishing farther offshore. Wind and waves inhibited many anglers from deep fishing trips, ultimately limiting their success. Coho salmon, Chinook salmon and brown trout were caught on jigs and spoons in 90-110 feet of water utilizing downriggers. 

Racine County

Anglers fishing from the Racine South Pier had some catches of coho. Some coho were caught casting spoons, but most were caught floating a tube jig with a piece of shrimp.

Sheboygan County

Pier/Shore: Fishing effort was extremely high on Friday and Saturday with many anglers targeting coho and Chinook salmon. Anglers who began their fishing trips in the early morning saw the greatest success on their trips. Light-colored spoons were a popular bait being used off both piers to land fish. Although many anglers were successful on their trips, other anglers ended their fishing trips with no fish. The North Pier's water temperature averaged 66 degrees Fahrenheit. Fishermen who went out on Sunday morning saw little success, even with many different baits being used. The anglers who walked off the pier with salmon were out early in the morning and had completed their fishing trips around 10 a.m.

Ramp: Friday and Saturday showed a lot of success from anglers fishing off boats operating with downriggers, spoons and jigs. Salmon and trout were a highly sought after fish species, with many anglers coming in with their limits. A few anglers targeted brown trout and were successful. Low fishing effort was observed on Sunday with boats coming in with only 1-2 fish.
