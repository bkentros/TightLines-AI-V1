# PierCast Scoring v3 — Pass 1 Research and Calibration Foundation

## Executive decision

PierCast Scoring v3 Pass 1 is complete as a disabled nine-city, thirteen-species research package: 117 city/species pairings. The final decision contains 36 Grade A/B core-salmonid admissions, 29 Grade C nonnumeric deferrals, and 52 Grade D exclusions. The [completion report](PASS1_COMPLETION_REPORT.md) is authoritative. No live or shadow v2 score is changed by this package.

The pre-v3 baseline contained 44 provisional numeric curves. No current curve was grandfathered into v3: 36 received explicit mode reconstruction, while eight Grade C secondary-species curves were retired from the v3 numeric candidate set pending their named evidence gaps.

The renovation separates three questions that the current one-dimensional curve partially combines:

1. **How strong is this city for this species?** This is an absolute, cross-city fishery-strength judgment supported by pier-mode evidence.
2. **When is that opportunity reachable from the main piers?** This is a city/species seasonal-availability curve.
3. **Does the modeled nearshore temperature support the active seasonal mode?** This is a mode-specific thermal response, not one universal annual curve.

This separation is required for a reliable year-round product. A spring coho nearshore fishery, a summer upwelling opportunity, and a fall harbor-staging fishery can involve the same species but respond differently to the same surface temperature. Temperature may strengthen an already supported opportunity; it may not create a fishery that the seasonal and local evidence do not support.

## Scope

The current Pass 1 scope is:

- Ludington, Grand Haven, Manistee, and Frankfort–Elberta, Michigan;
- Sheboygan, Port Washington, Milwaukee, Racine, and Kenosha, Wisconsin; and
- all thirteen species in the PierCast catalog.

The city boundary is intentionally practical: one general reading for the named harbor and its listed main public fishing piers. It is not a prediction for a particular casting position or depth. It also is not permission to transfer county-wide, river, tributary, charter, or offshore catch into a pier score.

The machine-readable baseline is generated by `scripts/generate-pier-cast-v3-pass1-inventory.ts`. It fails unless runtime configuration contains exactly nine unique cities, thirteen species per city, 117 total decisions, and 44 currently configured curves.

The baseline reconciliation currently contains 44 legacy admissions, 21 evidence-gap deferrals, and 52 current-scope exclusions. These are not final v3 decisions. They ensure that every existing claim and every rejected pairing has an explicit starting position before the source refresh.

## Score semantics

The future FinFindr Opportunity Rating remains an absolute cross-city scale. It answers:

> Given the historically supported fishery, the active seasonal opportunity, and current modeled nearshore thermal fit, how worthwhile is it to target this identified species from the city's main public pier context?

It does not answer how many fish are present, the probability of catching one, whether a pier is open or safe, or whether regulations allow a particular method. Those claims require different data and remain separate.

A stronger fishery must remain stronger under equivalent seasonal and temperature conditions. It earns a higher peak potential, a wider productive window, or both. The model will support the complete 1–10 range, but no city or species receives a Prime quota. A `10` is reserved for a reference-class fishery during exceptional alignment.

## Evidence hierarchy

Pass 1 uses sources in the following order:

1. State DNR creel estimates that identify location, fishing mode, species, effort, catch or harvest, and time period.
2. Dated DNR field or weekly reports that identify the city and pier/shore mode.
3. Multi-year DNR county-by-mode harvest tables, used for recurrence and bounded relative strength.
4. NOAA, USGS, university, and Sea Grant research for environmental representation and fish biology.
5. Municipal or land-manager sources for the identity and public context of the main piers.

Wisconsin DNR publishes county-by-fishing-mode salmonid harvest estimates through 2024. The tables provide a long recurrence history, but no city-specific pier effort denominator; they therefore cannot support a city CPUE calculation by themselves.[^1] Wisconsin's 2024 open-water report estimates 70,460 pier angler-hours and 8,223 harvested fish across its combined survey area. Its survey limitations and combined geography prevent those statewide rates from becoming a city score.[^2]

Michigan DNR's Great Lakes creel program interviews anglers about effort, target species, catch, and harvest and publishes a Great Lakes dataset.[^3] Its 2025 statewide program covered 84 locations and more than 20,000 angler parties, but statewide totals are context rather than evidence for a specific pier/month rating.[^4] Weekly reports reflect approximately the prior seven days and warn that Great Lakes conditions can change daily or hourly; they are event observations, not monthly catch rates.[^5]

Stocking supports continued presence only. Boat, charter, river, tributary, and offshore results are excluded from numeric pier magnitude unless a separate source establishes the transfer. Commercial guides, forums, videos, and social posts may identify a research lead but cannot admit or score a pairing.

## Evidence grades

Evidence confidence remains separate from the number:

| Grade | Required support | Numeric treatment |
|---|---|---|
| A | Repeated local pier-mode quantitative evidence with seasonal resolution and current exact-city corroboration | Eligible for Formula v3 shadow |
| B | Recurring local or county pier-mode evidence plus exact-city target evidence; one important denominator or seasonal detail is absent | Eligible for Formula v3 shadow with explicit limitation |
| C | Locally plausible and corroborated, but annual timing, magnitude, or adult pier thermal response depends heavily on transfer or judgment | Nonnumeric research; not promotion eligible |
| D | Species identity, geography, mode, recurrence, or targetability is unresolved | No numeric curve |

Confidence never silently lowers the score. A strong but Grade C hypothesis is withheld; it is not displayed as an artificially low score.

The reconciliation identifies 16 Grade A pairings, 20 Grade B pairings, and 29 Grade C pairings; 52 excluded pairings are Grade D. Final Pass 1 treatment admits all 36 Grade A/B core pairs and defers all 29 Grade C pairs without v3 numbers. The eight Grade C legacy numeric curves remain historical comparators only.

## Admission rules

Every city/species pairing must pass all of these gates before receiving a v3 curve:

1. The species is identified rather than grouped as “bass,” “trout,” or “whitefish.”
2. Evidence applies to the city harbor or main-pier context.
3. Pier or comparable city shoreline mode is supported.
4. Opportunity is recurring and intentionally targetable, not one incidental catch.
5. A complete annual availability curve can be defended without treating unobserved months as measured absence.
6. At least one applicable thermal mode can be supported.
7. Current access and regulation context has been reviewed.

All thirteen species receive a decision at every city. “Not scored” is a deliberate result, not missing work.

## Opportunity-mode model

Each admitted pairing will contain one or more opportunity modes. A mode represents a biologically and behaviorally coherent fishery such as spring nearshore coho, summer upwelling steelhead, or fall harbor-staging Chinook.

Each mode requires:

- an absolute fishery-strength value on the common 1–10 scale;
- a continuous annual seasonal-availability curve;
- a mode-specific thermal-response curve;
- covered methods and main-pier context;
- evidence identifiers for geography, timing, targetability, and magnitude;
- an evidence grade and explicit limitations.

The research representation is:

```text
mode seasonal potential = 1 + (fishery strength - 1) × seasonal availability
city/species seasonal potential = strongest eligible mode potential
```

`Fishery strength` is the absolute ceiling justified for that city/species/mode. `Seasonal availability` is a continuous `0–1` annual function describing how fully that mode is active on the date. Using the maximum rather than adding modes prevents overlapping spring, summer, or staging hypotheses from stacking into an unsupported score.

Each mode must also declare whether its thermal response is mode-specific or inherited from the shared species response. Mode-specific curves are permitted only when the evidence supports a meaningful difference. A new schema is not permission to invent extra coefficients.

The initial mode catalog is a research taxonomy, not a scoring artifact. A candidate mode is deleted when local evidence does not support it. Multiple modes prevent the engine from interpreting, for example, a cold spring surface temperature and a fall staging temperature as the same biological situation.

Michigan Sea Grant describes spring thermal fronts, summer upwelling, and declining temperature importance as fish stage near river mouths in fall. That is direct support for testing season-aware thermal modes rather than a universal annual response.[^6] It is not enough to assign a local magnitude; local creel and pier evidence still owns that value. USGS research also demonstrates seasonal differences in salmonine energetics, reinforcing that temperature effects should not be treated as seasonally interchangeable.[^7]

## Absolute magnitude rubric

| Potential | Meaning under fully active season and supportive conditions |
|---:|---|
| 1.0–2.0 | Negligible or rare intentional main-pier opportunity |
| 2.1–4.0 | Limited or inconsistent opportunity |
| 4.1–6.0 | Real but ordinary targetable opportunity |
| 6.1–8.0 | Strong recurring fishery |
| 8.1–9.4 | Excellent recurring regional fishery |
| 9.5–10.0 | Reference-class opportunity under exceptional alignment |

Magnitude will be calibrated lake-wide, not independently within each city. The research record must explain why a city/species mode is stronger or weaker than the reference modes immediately above and below it. Stocking volume, popularity, and a visually appealing score are never sufficient reasons.

## Legacy-score diagnostic

The generated legacy audit evaluates every current curve on all 365 dates in a common reference year. It is descriptive and leaves v2 immutable.

The first audit confirms:

- 44 current numeric curves require v3 review;
- eight are numeric despite Grade C evidence;
- 40 cannot display `10.0/10` under Formula v2 even at perfect thermal suitability; and
- Sheboygan Chinook currently reaches an excellent/reference-class seasonal peak on Grade B evidence because no city-specific pier effort denominator exists.

These findings do not mean 40 curves should be inflated or that Sheboygan Chinook should automatically be reduced. They identify the claims that require the most careful cross-city calibration. The v3 goal is a mathematically available full scale with evidence-controlled access to its upper end—not equal peaks or a guaranteed 10 for every species.

The [P0 claim review](P0_CLAIM_REVIEW.md) rechecks the nine highest-risk legacy claims against their underlying DNR event evidence. All nine remain valid research candidates, but their legacy magnitudes are quarantined as comparators. In particular, occurrence-based evidence may retain a species while still requiring a lower or otherwise different v3 peak.

The [core cross-city diagnostic](CORE_CROSS_CITY_DIAGNOSTIC.md) then reviews the four species configured at all nine cities as lake-wide comparison sets. It identifies provisional reference candidates and the legacy duration anomalies that must be resolved before any v3 strength value is approved.

## Calibration procedure

### Stage 1 — Source normalization

Every retained observation is normalized into a ledger row containing publisher, title, publication date, retrieval date, URL or archived file, location resolution, fishing mode, species, time resolution, metric, value, denominator, survey limitations, and permitted use.

The same source may support multiple claims, but each use is recorded separately. Estimated zero harvest is retained as a zero estimate with its sampling frame; it is not rewritten as fish absence. Winter gaps are recorded as gaps.

### Stage 2 — Pair disposition

All 117 pairings receive an independent admit, defer, or exclude decision. Existing runtime inheritance labels are historical inputs only. The 44 configured pairings are not automatically admitted, and the 73 unconfigured pairings are not automatically excluded.

### Stage 3 — Mode construction

For admitted pairings, dated local observations establish possible mode timing. Repeated, effort-aware evidence establishes magnitude. Regional biology may interpolate within a local window but cannot create a local mode.

Seasonal availability must be continuous across December 31 and January 1. Where public access, regulation, ice, or open-water availability blocks the practical fishery, that state remains a separate eligibility gate rather than a hidden biological coefficient.

### Stage 4 — Cross-city calibration

Modes are reviewed by species across all nine cities at once. Reviewers see the evidence table before the proposed number and must document every material ordering. The highest reference modes are assigned first; other modes are located relative to those anchors.

This stage specifically tests whether better fisheries remain better, whether weaker ports are being inflated to use the scale, and whether differences are being inferred from harvest totals that lack comparable effort.

### Stage 5 — Thermal research

Temperature response is assigned by species and opportunity mode. It is not city-specific unless reliable evidence demonstrates a regional biological difference. City-specific model-cell representation remains a separate validation problem.

NOAA's LMHOFS supplies model-generated temperature guidance to 120 hours four times daily.[^8] NOAA explicitly describes it as hydrodynamic-model guidance, not a pier measurement. The system's 50 m to 2.5 km horizontal resolution also does not establish accuracy at a specific harbor edge.[^9]

### Stage 6 — Holdout review

Where enough years exist, at least one recent season is withheld from curve construction and used to test peak timing, cross-city ordering, and false high-opportunity windows. Where holdout data do not exist, the pair remains Grade B or C and the limitation is explicit.

## Required Pass 1 artifacts

The completed pass contains:

- a 117-row pairing inventory and decision matrix;
- a normalized evidence ledger with claim-level source use;
- an admitted-mode file with fishery strength, availability, thermal hypotheses, confidence, and citations;
- a cross-city calibration table for each species;
- a gap register naming the exact evidence needed for deferred pairs;
- a full-year weekly review table;
- a calibration audit documenting anchors, reversals, and rejected interpretations; and
- generated runtime configuration candidates that remain disabled until Pass 2.

## Pass 1 completion gates

Pass 1 is complete only when:

- all 117 pair decisions have evidence and rationale;
- every admitted mode has geography, timing, targetability, and magnitude support;
- every numeric value is traceable to a calibration judgment and source set;
- no `7.0+` mode lacks strong recurring evidence;
- no `9.5+` mode lacks reference-class evidence;
- winter and survey gaps are explicit;
- all curves are continuous and cover the full year;
- every species has been calibrated across cities rather than city by city;
- an independent fisheries specialist review packet is complete and external sign-off is required before Pass 2 promotion; and
- the existing v2 configuration and archives remain unchanged.
