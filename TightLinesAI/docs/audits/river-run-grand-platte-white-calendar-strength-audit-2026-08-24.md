# Grand, Platte, and White Calendar and Run-Strength Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-24\
**Scope:** All nine fall Chinook, Coho, and Steelhead profiles\
**Runtime status:** Hidden owner review; no public enablement or deployment

## Decision summary

The nine profiles were rechecked against direct Michigan DNR weir/passage
records, the current DNR stocking database, current fishery assessments and
field observations, species biology, passage endpoints, and the five accepted
Michigan comparison rivers. Egg-collection dates were separated from migration
dates, stocking was treated as context rather than an adult-return forecast, and
upstream ladder counts were not extrapolated to the whole river.

## Why Grand increased without becoming a signature 9–10 profile

Grand Chinook are directly stocked in the lower system (about 150,000 in 2023
and 2025), DNR describes an annual salmon run, and historic lower-river catches
were much larger than the 233 fish counted at Webber in 2008. Webber is 102
river miles upstream, after multiple structures; DNR says passage declines at
successive ladders. Treating its count as the whole Grand understated the run,
but the evidence does not support equating Grand Chinook with the accepted 10/10
Pere Marquette, Betsie, or Big Manistee signature fisheries. The balanced result
is 7/10 and Sectional.

Grand Steelhead likewise should not have been based mainly on 164 fall fish at
Webber. DNR says most Webber Steelhead passage occurs in spring, current
stocking is roughly 22,000–32,000 annually, current enforcement observations
describe numerous Grand Rapids spring-run patrols, and older portfolio evidence
found Grand catch rates above Betsie and Muskegon in the comparison year.
Because this product profile is fall entry—not the entire annual fishery—the
corrected ceiling is 7/10 rather than 8–10. September passage 102 miles upstream
also supports sparse lower-river presence beginning Sep. 15.

Grand Coho remains 8/10: approximately 179,000–236,000 have been stocked in each
recent complete year; historic Webber passage exceeded Grand Chinook and
Steelhead; and September was the main passage month with a long November
component. The sub-1% historic return evidence prevents a speculative increase
to 9 or 10.

## Why Platte moved earlier

The 1990 DNR report is direct migration evidence. Coho concentrated off Platte
Bay during the last two weeks of August, migrated upriver in September, peaked
Sep. 15, and 71% of harvested Coho were taken Sep. 17–23. The previous Oct. 22
anchor came from a 2025 egg-collection window and was not a valid migration
peak. The corrected opportunity anchor is Sep. 20, centered on the dominant Sep.
17–23 interval; October is tapering/maturation context.

The same report places the Chinook peak on Sep. 23 and says 85% of the run was
harvested Sep. 23–Oct. 17. That supports a Sep. 15 sparse start and Sep. 23
anchor, while the unstocked/secondary nature of this recurring run keeps 4/10.

Platte Steelhead were strong throughout fall, peaked the week of Oct. 17, and
the 3,016-fish 1990 return was the largest in its 1980–90 lower-weir record.
Recent stocking is about 19,000–23,000 annually, comparable with accepted 7/10
Betsie and White stocking scale. Concentrated scope distinguishes the short
lower corridor from strength; the ceiling is therefore 7/10.

## Why White stayed where it is

The 2026 DNR assessment calls the lower White a destination fishery for Chinook
and Steelhead, documents annual migrations of all three species, describes good
natural Chinook reproduction, recommends about 20,000 stocked Steelhead per
year, and says Coho are only occasionally caught. That supports 7/10 Chinook,
2/10 Coho, and 7/10 Steelhead.

The 1983 White weir was installed Aug. 15, but ineffective gear prevented a
daily early-run count. It does not prove a large Aug. 15 return; combined with
statewide Chinook biology it supports retaining Aug. 15 as a deliberately low
5%-of-ceiling beginning. The improved October operation caught most sampled
Chinook Oct. 9–14, consistent with the Oct. 8–15 peak window. The same report
says the river Steelhead fishery replaces Chinook in late October and
intensifies until early December, closely matching the existing Oct. 20 start
and Nov. 25 anchor.

## Cross-river ceiling controls

Ceilings were compared with the accepted portfolio, but were not copied:

- Chinook: PM 10, Big Manistee 10, Muskegon 9, Betsie 10, St. Joseph 3.
- Coho: PM 6, Big Manistee 5, Muskegon 3, Betsie 3, St. Joseph 7.
- Steelhead: PM 8, Big Manistee 8, Muskegon 9, Betsie 7, St. Joseph 9.

The comparison considered adult passage/catch evidence, natural reproduction,
current stocking, destination status, passage attrition, corridor length,
distribution, season scope, and evidence age. It did not map stocking totals
directly to ceilings.
