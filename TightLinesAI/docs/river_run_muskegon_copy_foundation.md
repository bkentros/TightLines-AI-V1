## 1. Normative public geography

Use these exact section names in every River Run primitive:

1. **Lower river (Muskegon Lake–M-120)**
2. **Middle river (M-120–Newaygo)**
3. **Upper river (Newaygo–Croton Dam)**
4. **Muskegon Lake, Lake Michigan channel, and river entrance** — staging
   context only; this is not part of the Lower river and does not prove river
   entry.

The Croton Dam area is an emphasis inside the Upper river, not a fourth public
section. Building copy must tell users to check it for early arrivals while
preserving the stronger lakeward and middle-river interpretation of new entry.
`Upper river` never means water above Croton Dam.

Stage Where to Start names one primary section and may name one secondary
section with a clear reason. Habitat and tactics belong in Guide's Read.

## 2. Barrier inventory

### Croton Dam

Croton Dam is the first upstream fish-passage barrier. Chinook, Coho, and
Steelhead migrating from Lake Michigan and Muskegon Lake cannot pass it.
River Run must never recommend water above it or imply passage.

- Barrier ID: `muskegon_croton_dam`
- Passage status: impassable for all three target species
- Public upstream limit: yes
- Copy phrase: `below Croton Dam` or `Upper river (Newaygo–Croton Dam)`
- Safety: do not invent a fixed closure distance; follow current signs, booms,
  DNR notices, and dam-safety information.

### Former Newaygo Dam

Newaygo Dam was completely removed in 1969. It is not a current barrier,
tailwater, or fishery endpoint. The city and public access remain valid section
boundaries, but public copy must never say fish accumulate `below Newaygo Dam`.

## 3. Section evidence and landmarks

The DNR's 2022–2023 creel survey formally separates Croton Dam–Newaygo and
Newaygo–M-120. DNR access guidance identifies Muskegon Lake–Newaygo as the
deeper boat-oriented corridor and Newaygo–Croton as the shallower, swifter
section. The owner approved M-120 and Newaygo as recognizable public boundaries.

Approved landmarks are Muskegon Lake, M-120, Newaygo, and Croton Dam. Individual
access sites such as Maple Island, Bridgeton, Felch Street, Henning Park, Pine
Street, and Charles Besemer Park may support research but should not replace the
three section names in routine state copy.

Sources:

## 4. Source coverage

USGS 04121970 is on the right bank below Croton Drive, about 1,000 feet below
Croton Dam. It supplies accepted discharge and measured water temperature.
Flow is completely regulated by Croton Dam. It directly represents the Croton
Dam area, not the whole Upper river and never the Middle river, Lower river,
Muskegon Lake, channel, or river entrance.

USGS 04122001 at Bridge Street in Newaygo is a partial-record station that
publishes discharge only above its high-stage threshold. It is context only and
powers no primitive. Retired station 04122000 must not be called current.

| Migration Timing | Season-to-date Croton-area evidence |
| Push | Current fresh-movement support at the Croton Dam area |
| Fishability | Current presentation shape at the Croton Dam area |

## 5. Species truths and terminal behavior

Accepted calendars, thresholds, ceilings, and determinations remain unchanged
unless a separate scoring defect is proven.

### Chinook

- Strong, broad fall opportunity; 90/100 river ceiling.
- Muskegon Lake and channel are staging context before dependable river entry.
- Croton-area accumulation becomes useful during Building and strongest later.
- Terminal state: `Fall run complete`, no score, and return when staging begins
  in early August.

### Coho

- Limited, sectional fall opportunity; 30/100 river ceiling.
- Copy must not inflate this into a dependable river-wide Coho run.
- Croton may hold early arrivals, but empty good-looking sections remain likely.
- Terminal state: `Fall run complete`, no score, and return when staging begins
  in early September.

### Steelhead

- Strong fall-entry opportunity; 90/100 river ceiling.
- September fish are `early Steelhead`; timing alone does not establish a
  separate strain or summer run.
- Terminal state: `Fall entry complete`, no score, no winter recommendation,
  and return when fall-entry monitoring resumes in early September.

## 6. State-copy contract

### Migration Stage

- Before entry: staging context only.
- Beginning: Lower river primary; Middle river may be conditional.
- Building: Middle river primary while explicitly checking the Croton Dam area
  in the Upper river for early arrivals. Later Building may make the Upper river
  primary without implying equal distribution.
- Peak: Upper river primary with Croton-area emphasis; Middle river may be the
  comparison for fresher fish.
- Tapering and Ending: Upper river primary; add Middle only when justified.
- Complete: no active starting section.

### Migration Timing

Ahead, Typical, Delayed, mixed, reversal-tempered, evaluating, insufficient,
and complete states must say they use season-to-date Croton flow and measured
temperature. Timing may adjust expectations among approved sections but does
not locate fish or prove movement.

### Push

Why copy uses at most three short points: measured Croton hydraulic response,
measured temperature, and the material rain/cap/freshness qualification. Push
does not prove arrivals or describe conditions downstream.

### Fishability

Why copy normally uses three short points: flow band, direction/freshness, and
Croton-area scope. It determines presentation shape, not abundance, activity,
access, or safety.

### Activity

Why copy uses at most three points: overall responsiveness, strongest or tied
time block, and lifecycle/confidence/scope. A block is strongest only with at
least a three-point lead. All measured-river claims are Croton-area claims.

### Fish In River

The internal score remains exact. The public score is state-preserving and
rounded to the nearest five. It estimates seasonal presence relative to the
species-specific Muskegon ceiling; it is not a count, live report, or claim of
equal distribution.

## 7. Copy QA prohibitions

Reject Muskegon public copy containing:

- foreign river names or landmarks;
- `Croton tailwater` as a fourth public section;
- `Newaygo Dam` as an active structure;
- vague lower/middle/upper language without the approved first-use range;
- a recommendation above Croton Dam or an implication of fish passage;
- Croton measurements applied downstream;
- Steelhead winter-holding, winter-experience, strain, or summer-run claims;
- more than three Why points or a strongest Activity block with a lead under
  three points;
- a score after a completed salmon run or completed Steelhead fall entry.

## 8. Current regulation boundary

The 2026 Michigan regulations list Croton Dam downstream to M-120 as Type 3,
year-round trout and salmon water with a Muskegon-specific Rainbow Trout limit
and special artificial-lure rules from August 1 through November 15. The Lower
river below M-120 is not silently assigned the same reach-specific rule.
Regulations, access, closures, and posted dam boundaries must be rechecked at
release time.

Source: [2026 Michigan Fishing Regulations](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/LED/digests/2026-Michigan-Fishing-Regulations_web_accessible.pdf?rev=b8a345e6033d4f5e9ddfc8610dec6e1d)

## 9. Implementation acceptance

The bundled renovation is implemented under copy version
`river-run-copy-v32` for Chinook, Coho, and Steelhead across all six
primitives. The dedicated Muskegon copy audit covers 246 production-derived
scenarios and enforces geography, barrier, source-scope, copy-length,
three-point Why, Activity-leader, terminal-state, foreign-river, and public
presence-rounding rules.

Underlying calendars, presence ceilings, hydraulic thresholds, Fishability
bands, Push scoring, Activity weights, lifecycle constraints, and condition
sources were not changed. The only lifecycle behavior change is removal of the
unimplemented public Steelhead winter handoff: December 23 is now the scoreless
`Fall entry complete` state.
